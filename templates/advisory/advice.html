    📚 Understanding Your Irrigation Advice
    Key terms and how they're calculated
    🎯 Confidence Score

    What it means: A measure of how reliable our irrigation recommendation is, expressed as a percentage from 0-100%.

    How it's calculated: Based on data quality, weather forecast accuracy, satellite image clarity, and crop model validation.

    Interpretation:
    • 80-100%: High confidence - follow recommendations closely
    • 60-79%: Good confidence - recommendations are reliable
    • 40-59%: Moderate confidence - use with field observations
    • Below 40%: Low confidence - verify with local conditions
    💧 Water Stress

    What it means: Indicates how much your crops are struggling due to insufficient water availability.

    How it's calculated: Ratio of actual crop water consumption to optimal water needs, considering soil moisture and rainfall.

    Scale:
    • 0.0-0.3: Low stress - crops are well-watered
    • 0.4-0.7: Moderate stress - irrigation may be beneficial
    • 0.8-1.0: High stress - irrigation urgently needed
    🌵 Drought Risk

    What it means: Probability of drought conditions affecting your field in the coming weeks.

    How it's calculated: Combines rainfall forecasts, soil moisture trends, and historical drought patterns for your location.

    Risk Levels:
    • 0.0-0.3: Low risk - normal precipitation expected
    • 0.4-0.7: Moderate risk - monitor conditions closely
    • 0.8-1.0: High risk - prepare drought management strategies
    🌱 Crop Coefficient (Kc)

    What it means: A factor that relates your crop's water needs to the reference evapotranspiration (ET₀).

    How it's calculated: Based on crop type, growth stage, and development phase using FAO-56 methodology.

    Typical Values:
    • 0.3-0.7: Initial/establishment stage
    • 0.7-1.2: Development and mid-season
    • 0.6-1.0: Late season and maturity
    ☀️ Reference Evapotranspiration (ET₀)

    What it means: The amount of water that would evaporate from soil and transpire from a reference grass crop (mm/day).

    How it's calculated: Using the Penman-Monteith equation with temperature, humidity, wind speed, and solar radiation data.

    Typical Values:
    • 2-4 mm/day: Cool, humid conditions
    • 4-6 mm/day: Moderate temperature, average humidity
    • 6-8+ mm/day: Hot, dry, windy conditions
    🌍 Soil Moisture (NDMI)

    What it means: Normalized Difference Moisture Index - indicates soil and vegetation water content from satellite imagery.

    How it's calculated: Using near-infrared and shortwave infrared bands from Sentinel-2 satellite: (NIR - SWIR) / (NIR + SWIR).

    Index Values:
    • 0.6-1.0: Very high moisture content
    • 0.2-0.6: Moderate to high moisture
    • 0.0-0.2: Low moisture content
    • Below 0.0: Very dry conditions
    🔬 Data Integration Process

    Our recommendations combine multiple Earth observation datasets and agronomic models:

    • Weather Analysis: Real-time and forecast data from multiple sources
    • Soil Assessment: Satellite-derived moisture and field characteristics
    • Crop Modeling: FAO-56 dual crop coefficient approach
    • Risk Analysis: Statistical models for drought and stress assessment
    • Quality Control: Data validation and uncertainty quantification
    • Local Calibration: Adjustments based on field-specific conditions
